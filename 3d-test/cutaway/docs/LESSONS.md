# Lessons

Every mistake below became one line in the job description. The next animal starts with all of them.

| What happened | What it looked like | The line it added |
|---|---|---|
| The tarantula's checklist said "thin bristles" | Quills twice the length they should be | Bristles and spikes are 0.15 to 0.25 of the limb's thickness |
| A rule kept the whole octopus in frame | The camera pulled back to a flat view from above, arms tucked in: a toy | Only the part being opened has to stay in view |
| A moth, a hydra and a fly graded themselves dense enough | Clean white diagrams. The moth was thrown out after 9 rounds | Density gets measured: dark share at least 0.8 of the reference's |
| The piranha passed on density in round 2 | A crocodile: jaws at 0.52 of the body length against 0.37 in the picture | Proportions get measured off the picture before the first line of code |
| A flying octopus and a scorpion-squid | Wings and tentacles fighting for the same half second | One main set of limbs per animal |
| Glow dots sprinkled over shells to add accents | Polka dots: the look that sank the moth | Green only as lines, never isolated dots |
| A film ported from another animal's choreography with an extra rule | Far away, flat, a toy | Port the closest approved film 1:1 and change the body only |
| A scarab with no close body | 4 rounds, about 450,000 tokens, still busy and cheap | An animal with no close body gets flagged before it starts |
| Every beat filmed from one side | The camera sat between 20° and 50°, at one height and one distance, for 4 beats | Every beat gets its own side, height and distance, aimed at its part |
| Short versions made by cutting the long film's busiest moments | Jumps from fragment to fragment | A short version is its own script with its own opening and exit |
| 4 animals at once, reviewed after round 6 | One moth alone ate about 430,000 tokens before it was dropped | 2 animals at a time, reviewed after round 2 |
